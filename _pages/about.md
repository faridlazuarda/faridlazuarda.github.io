I’m a PhD student at the [University of Edinburgh](https://www.ed.ac.uk/), where I’m very fortunate to be advised by [Edoardo Ponti](https://ducdauge.github.io/), [Alexandra Birch](https://sites.google.com/view/alexandra-birch/), and [Ivan Titov](http://ivan-titov.org/). I’m a member of the [Institute for Language, Cognition and Computation (ILCC)](https://informatics.ed.ac.uk/ilcc) and the [Edinburgh NLP Group](https://edinburghnlp.inf.ed.ac.uk/). From September to December 2026 I’m a visiting researcher at [EPFL](https://www.epfl.ch/), hosted by [Antoine Bosselut](https://atcbosselut.github.io/).

My research asks a big question: <strong>"What should a model remember, and how can it keep that memory under a tight compute budget?"</strong> I work on <strong>long-term memory mechanisms in world models</strong>, <strong>efficient inference</strong>, and <strong>evaluation</strong>. Currently, I study <strong>adaptive memory compression</strong> for long-context models, and I am building a culturally grounded video and world-model benchmark supported by a Google DeepMind gift and an NVIDIA compute award.

Before Edinburgh, I was a Research Assistant at [MBZUAI](https://mbzuai.ac.ae/) in Abu Dhabi and a visiting researcher at the [UKP Lab, TU Darmstadt](https://www.informatik.tu-darmstadt.de/ukp), where I worked on multilingual, multicultural, and parameter-efficient NLP. Those are topics I still enjoy discussing!

I know how much research success can depend on access and opportunity. If you’re from an underrepresented group and think a conversation might help, feel free to reach out by [email](mailto:farid.adilazuarda@ed.ac.uk).
